    The château forecourt looks onto a castle rising straight out of its moat, the home of the princes of Condé for generations.

    What you see is two buildings. The Petit Château dates from the Renaissance. The Grand Château was destroyed during the Revolution and rebuilt from 1875 to the early 1880s by the architect Honoré Daumet for Henri d'Orléans, Duke of Aumale, a son of King Louis-Philippe, to show his collections.

    The estate came to the powerful Montmorency family in 1484. The Constable Anne de Montmorency, one of the great men of the sixteenth-century court, had the old fortress modernised and built the Petit Château for himself in the mid sixteenth century; some sources give 1551, others around 1560, and the architect was probably Jean Bullant. From the Montmorencys it passed, through Charlotte de Montmorency, to her son the Grand Condé.
