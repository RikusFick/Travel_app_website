    The pomegranate city

    Read along

    You are beside the agora of Side, next to the theatre. The name Side means "pomegranate" in the city's ancient local language, and the pomegranate appears often on its buildings, inscriptions and decorations.

    The Greek historians say Side was founded by settlers from Kyme, a Greek city on the Aegean coast, more than two and a half thousand years ago. And the historian Arrian tells a curious story: as soon as the settlers landed, they forgot their Greek and began to speak a language of their own. Only about ten short inscriptions in this local language have been found, and their alphabet was first worked out in 1950.

    This square was the agora, the commercial marketplace. It has a dark side too. In the first century BC, pirates from Cilicia, further east along the coast, used Side as a harbour and slave market. According to the Greek geographer Strabo, they sold their captives here by auction. The Romans eventually cleared the seas of the pirates, and Side entered its richest era.

    In the middle of the square stand the remains of a round temple, probably dedicated to Tyche, the goddess of fortune. Part of it has been rebuilt from its original blocks.
