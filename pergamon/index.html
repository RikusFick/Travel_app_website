    A royal city in the sky

    This part of the route looks at A royal city in the sky.

    Free sample: stop 1

    Play0:00 / –:––
    Read along

    You are at the top of the Acropolis of Pergamon. Look down. The modern town of Bergama lies far below you, and beyond it the wide plain of the Bakırçay river. Up here, on this steep hill, kings once lived.

    Pergamon became the capital of a Greek royal family, the Attalids, in the third century BC, in the turbulent years after Alexander the Great. They were ambitious, rich and clever. They turned this rocky hill into one of the most spectacular cities of the ancient world, with temples, palaces, a theatre, a library and a great altar arranged in terraces down the slopes.

    The Attalids wanted Pergamon to be a centre of learning and the arts, competing with the other great Hellenistic kingdoms. Their sculptors created a dramatic style, full of movement and emotion, long called the "Pergamon style".

    The story of the dynasty ended in a surprising way. In 133 BC, the last king, Attalus the Third, died and left his entire kingdom to Rome in his will. Pergamon went on to become a great city of the Roman province of Asia.

    UNESCO listed "Pergamon and its Multi-Layered Cultural Landscape" as a World Heritage Site in 2014.
