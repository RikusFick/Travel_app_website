    Volta's lake

    Start on Como's promenade beside the neoclassical Volta Temple, honouring physicist Alessandro Volta and the glacial Y-shaped lake.

    Free sample: stop 1

    Play0:00 / –:––
    Read along

    You're looking at Lake Como. The Romans called it Larius, and some locals still call it Lario. It was carved by an ancient glacier into the shape of an upside-down Y, and in places it's more than four hundred metres deep.

    The round, temple-like building here honours Como's most famous son: Alessandro Volta, born here in 1745. Volta invented the first electric battery, the voltaic pile. Every time you say "volt", you say his name.

    The Tempio Voltiano was built for the hundredth anniversary of his death and opened in 1928. Its architect, Federico Frigerio, modelled it on the Pantheon in Rome. Inside are Volta's own instruments, including early versions of his battery, and a long series of showcases traces his life and the honours he received.

    Julius Caesar renamed the Roman town here Novum Comum, "New Como", in 59 BC.
