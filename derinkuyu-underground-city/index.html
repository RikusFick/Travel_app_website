    A city beneath your feet

    You are at the entrance to Derinkuyu Underground City. Look around at the town above ground. Beneath your feet, hidden in the rock, is a second settlement: stables, kitchens, wine presses, churches and wells. The ministry gives the complex an approximate depth of about eighty-five metres. Other published figures range from forty to eighty-five metres, because researchers measure to different shafts and floors.

    The name Derinkuyu means "deep well" in Turkish, after the deep shafts that supplied water to the underground city and to the town above it. Its older name in Cappadocian Greek was Malakopi.

    Cappadocia holds many places like this. The Ministry of Culture estimates between 150 and 200 underground settlements in the region, from small hideaways to great complexes like this one. They were possible because of the rock. The ministry says most of these settlements were made by carving down into soft tuff, in a volcanic landscape shaped by erosion. UNESCO describes this as creating architecture by removing material, rather than by putting a building together. As in other typical underground cities, the entrances were hidden at the surface, and most underground cities have lost their original entrances.

    Why go underground? Above all, for safety. When danger came, people could vanish below with animals and food, close the doors, and wait. UNESCO records that in later periods, to resist Arab invasions, Christians banded together into subterranean towns such as Kaymaklı and Derinkuyu, which served as places of refuge. Underground it also stays cool in summer and warm in winter.

    The ministry says the underground city comprises hundreds of spaces linked by long galleries and complex tunnels. Only about a tenth of Derinkuyu can be visited. The city opened to the public in 1965. Eight levels have been cleared, and the area open to visitors is about two thousand five hundred square metres. How many levels there are in all, how far the city spreads, and how deep it goes are not known for certain.

    Derinkuyu and Kaymaklı belong to the UNESCO World Heritage Site "Göreme National Park and the Rock Sites of Cappadocia", inscribed in 1985.
