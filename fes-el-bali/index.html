    Bab Bou Jeloud, the Blue Gate

    On Place Bou Jeloud, outside the gate, looking through the central arch. This is stop 1 on the route.

    Free sample: stop 1

    Play0:00 / –:––
    Read along

    Beyond this gate lies the old city of Fez. UNESCO counts its medina among the largest and best-kept historic towns anywhere in the Arab and Muslim world, and lists it as a World Heritage Site. From here on, you travel on foot.

    The gate in front of you has a double history. An architectural archive dates it to 1913: a monumental gateway that took over the role of an older, simple gate to the west. A catalogue entry from Morocco's Ministry of Culture describes instead a twelfth-century gate, redecorated with glazed tiles in 1913. Either way, the tilework you see dates from 1913.

    The same archive measures it at about fifteen metres wide and four deep: two walls with a hollow between them, where big wooden doors swing back. Three horseshoe arches, a larger one in the middle, are flanked by blind arched windows. The arches have scalloped edges framed in glazed tile painted with flowers, and the whole gate is capped with crenellations. Notice the colours as you walk through: one face is mostly blue, the other mostly green.

    Look through the central arch. Framed inside it are two minarets. The larger belongs to the Bou Inania Madrasa, the smaller to the Sidi Lazzaz Mosque. The archive notes that this road was once one of the busiest in Fez, lined with fondouks, the sheds and warehouses of the caravan trade. Walk through the gate and bear left onto the main street, Tala'a Kebira.
