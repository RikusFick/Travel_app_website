  1. 1

    The museum at Fustat

    Outside the National Museum of Egyptian Civilization, beside the lake of Ain el-Sira in Fustat, Old Cairo. This is stop 1 on the route.

    Play0:00 / –:––
    Read along

    This is the National Museum of Egyptian Civilization, known by its initials, NMEC. It stands in Fustat, in Old Cairo, beside the lake of Ain el-Sira.

    The museum took a long time to build. Its foundation stone was laid in 2002. UNESCO gave technical help. A first exhibition hall opened in 2017, and the official inauguration took place on the third of April 2021.

    Its idea is wider than a museum of pharaohs. It sets out to tell the story of Egyptian civilization across all its ages. That story begins in prehistoric times. It moves on through the age of the pharaohs, then Greek, Coptic and Islamic Egypt, and on to the modern day. It even includes the traditional culture that Egyptians inherited, which the museum calls popular heritage.

    The site is large. Its total area is reported to be about one hundred and thirty-eight thousand square metres.

    But most visitors come for one part of it, the Royal Mummies Hall. The museum calls it the crown jewel of its collection. Its design is meant to feel like a walk through the Valley of the Kings. That is where most of these kings and queens were first laid to rest.

    The museum lists twenty royal mummies there, eighteen kings and two queens. They span the Seventeenth to the Twentieth Dynasty: the end of a divided age, and the whole of Egypt's New Kingdom.

    These are not objects in the usual sense. They are the bodies of people who believed that the body had to survive so that the soul could live on. They spent a large part of their wealth on that hope. The museum presents them in a dim, quiet hall, built for them alone.
