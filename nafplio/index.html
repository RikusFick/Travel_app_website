    The harbour and Bourtzi

    Read along

    Bourtzi, the little sea fort out on its rock, guards the mouth of the bay.

    The rock is the islet of Agioi Theodoroi, and the town says a Byzantine church of Saint Theodore once stood there.

    Together with the Five Brothers bastion on the shore, it controlled every ship that sailed into the harbour.

    Venice raised it in the fifteenth century.

    The Ministry of Culture says work began in June 1471, under the architect Antonio Gambello.

    The town's own account dates the first tower on the rock to 1473.

    Gambello went back to Venice a year later, and an engineer called Brancaleone took charge.

    The date of its completion went unrecorded.

    Its first name was Castel Pasqualigo, after Vittore Pasqualigo, the Venetian governor of Nafplio.

    The name Bourtzi comes from burç, a Turkish word for a fort, as early Ottoman speakers said it.

    In the revolution of 1821, Greeks called it the Sea Tower, or Kasteli.

    From the fort a long chain ran across to a pier near the Five Brothers bastion.

    In a siege the chain was lifted to just above the water, and no ship could reach the harbour.

    That device gave Nafplio another name, Porto Cadena, the harbour of the chain.

    From 1480 to 1515, every ship coming into the port had to bring a cargo of stones and drop it on the breakwater.

    Those big boulders, laid out in a ring, kept deep-draught ships at a distance.

    In 1826, during the civil war among the Greeks, the government took refuge here briefly, the town records.

    From 1865 the executioners who served the Palamidi prisons lived in the fort.

    In 1935 it passed to the national tourism organisation and became a small hotel with a restaurant.

    The German architect Wulf Schaefer carried out the conversion.
