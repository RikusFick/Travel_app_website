What to do in Kuşadası

This page is in English.

Ephesus, the town of Selçuk, the House of the Virgin Mary, the hillside village of Şirince, and a classic longer day to Priene, Miletus and Didyma.

Kuşadası is a coastal resort town on Turkey’s Aegean shore. The places most visitors come from here to see lie inland: Ephesus, the town of Selçuk just beside the ruins, the House of the Virgin Mary, the hillside village of Şirince, and, on a longer outing, the classical cities of Priene, Miletus and Didyma.

Start with Ephesus

Ephesus was a great Roman city of marble streets, and the walk through the ruins follows the slope of the ancient city downhill, from the Upper Gate to the Library of Celsus, the Great Theatre and Harbour Street. Along the way you pass the State Agora and the small theatre of the Odeon, the Temple of Domitian, and the pillars carved with Hercules at the top of Curetes Street. The Terrace Houses — luxurious residences on the hillside above that street — are part of the same city. Set aside about two and a half to three hours for the visit. The Church of Mary and the Stadium are optional extras if you have time.

Selçuk, just beside the ruins

Selçuk is only three kilometres from Ephesus, and it is worth a visit of its own. A single tall column in a field just outside the town centre marks the site of the Temple of Artemis, once a Wonder of the World. Around 550 BC a huge marble temple was begun here, partly paid for by King Croesus of Lydia; it was later rebuilt even larger. According to the Roman writer Pliny, the columns were about eighteen metres tall. The temple site is open-air and free to enter. On Ayasuluk Hill stand the Gate of Persecution, the Basilica of St John, and the citadel; the basilica and the Ephesus Museum are ticketed. In the town, the plan of the İsa Bey Mosque recalls the Umayyad Mosque of Damascus, and the piers of a Byzantine aqueduct still rise near the station, often with storks nesting on them.

The House of the Virgin Mary

On Nightingale Mountain near Selçuk is a small stone hillside chapel, Meryem Ana Evi, venerated as the Virgin Mary’s last home. It is a living place of prayer for Christians and Muslims. Tradition, and the visions of Anne Catherine Emmerich, led a search in 1891; whether Mary lived at Ephesus or in Jerusalem is still discussed. Popes and pilgrims have come to the house, and a spring and wishing wall stand nearby.

Şirince

About eight kilometres from Selçuk — and from ancient Ephesus below — Şirince is a village of white houses with tiled roofs, tucked into the hills. Greek records knew it as Oreon Efes, “Ephesus on the mountain.” In the nineteenth century it was a prosperous, mostly Greek Orthodox place. Today the lanes still hold historic churches, including the Church of St John the Baptist, built in 1805, and the church of Saint Demetrios on the hill at the entrance. The village is known for its fruit wines, a revival of an older wine-making tradition, and for a long story of Greek Orthodox farmers and Muslim families from across the Aegean. The Earwise guide to Şirince is free.

A longer day: Priene, Miletus and Didyma

If you have a longer day, Priene, Miletus and Didyma make a classic day trip from Kuşadası and Ephesus. Priene is a grid-planned Hellenistic city, with the columns of the Temple of Athena and a compact council house. Miletus, once a great Ionian port, has a sweeping Roman theatre and the Faustina Baths. Didyma holds the Temple of Apollo: towering Ionic columns, an inner oracle courtyard, and a carved Medusa head from the temple frieze.
