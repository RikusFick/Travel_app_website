    Through the Nuruosmaniye Gate

    Read along

    You are at the Nuruosmaniye Gate, the grand eastern entrance of the Grand Bazaar. Istanbul calls it the Kapalıçarşı, the Covered Market. In front of you is one of the largest and oldest covered markets in the world: more than sixty streets under one roof, thousands of shops, and well over five centuries of buying, selling and bargaining.

    Look up above the gate before you go in. You'll see an inscription and the coat of arms of the Ottoman state, a reminder that this was never just a market. For centuries it was a treasury, a bank, a meeting place and the beating commercial heart of an empire.

    It all started with one building. Just a few years after he conquered Constantinople in 1453, Sultan Mehmed the Second, Mehmed the Conqueror, ordered a strong, domed market hall built here. The rents it brought in went to support Hagia Sophia, which he had turned into a mosque. Around that one building, over the next two and a half centuries, the whole bazaar grew up, street by street and shop by shop.

    The word , which you'll hear often here, comes from the Persian word for cloth. It meant "the market of the cloth sellers".
