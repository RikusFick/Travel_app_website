    Read along

    Ahead of you, the path to the Monastery climbs about 1.6 kilometres into the mountains, by way of roughly eight hundred steps. Many of them were cut into the rock by the Nabataeans themselves. This was not simply a footpath. It was a processional way, used by people walking up to a sanctuary high above the city to gather, feast and worship.

    As you climb, watch the walls around you. Small niches with plain carved blocks inside appear here and there. These are baetyls, the stone symbols the Nabataeans used to represent their gods. Tomb facades, steps, cisterns and water channels line the route too. Over the centuries, the people of Petra turned this whole gorge into a sacred landscape.

    The rock changes colour as you go: deep red, then bands of orange, white and purple, then honey-coloured as you get higher. Take it slowly. Every turn of the stairway opens a new view back down to the valley floor, and each one is a good excuse to stop.
