    Guimarães Castle stands on its hill with its granite walls and towers wrapped around a tall central keep.

    It began in the tenth century with Countess Mumadona Dias. After her husband's death she governed his lands. Around the year 950 she founded a monastery in the valley below, and a few years later she built a castle up here, dedicated to Saint Mamede, to protect the monastery and the people settling around it. A document of 968 says she built it against raiders, probably Norsemen. That first castle was probably made of wood, set among these rocks, and nothing of it survives.

    Over the centuries the walls were rebuilt again and again. At the end of the eleventh century, large stones were laid at the base of the north wall, the side facing the field of São Mamede, and traces of them can still be seen. In the twelfth century, in the time of Afonso Henriques, who became Portugal's first king, a new wall gave the castle roughly the outline it has now. Then, in the thirteenth or early fourteenth century, eight turrets were added to the walls and the keep was built. After that the castle took the shape it has today.

    It was besieged more than once. In 1369 King Henry the Second of Castile surrounded it, but its defenders held out. In 1385, during the crisis over the succession to the throne, the castle's commander sided with Castile, and this time it was the forces of King João the First who besieged the town and castle, which in the end surrendered to him.

    After the early sixteenth century the castle lost its military importance and fell into decay. From the seventeenth century its stones were taken to build elsewhere, and later it also served as a prison, until the end of the nineteenth century. In 1881 it was classed as a monument, and in 1910 as a National Monument. The state restored it in the 1930s, and it was inaugurated again on the fourth of June 1940, at the opening of the national centenary celebrations.
