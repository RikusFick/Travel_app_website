    Read along

    You're looking at the Museo Egizio, the Egyptian Museum of Turin.

    Founded in 1824 by King Charles Felix of Savoy, it's the oldest museum in the world devoted entirely to ancient Egypt. It holds tens of thousands of objects.

    Why Turin? The story starts with one strange object: a bronze tablet covered in Egyptian-style figures, which reached Turin in 1630. It fascinated the dukes of Savoy so much that in the 1750s King Charles Emmanuel the Third sent a scholar, Vitaliano Donati, to Egypt to collect antiquities. He came back with about three hundred pieces, from sites including Karnak and Coptos.

    That bronze tablet turned out not to be Egyptian at all. It's a Roman imitation, made around the first century AD. It's known as the Mensa Isiaca, and it's also named after Cardinal Bembo.
