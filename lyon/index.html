    Read along

    The Fourvière esplanade looks down over Lyon, where the rivers Saône and Rhône meet.

    This hill is where the Romans founded the city of Lugdunum in 43 BC, and it became a capital of Roman Gaul. The name Fourvière is thought to come from the Latin forum vetus, the old forum. On a clear day the Alps can be seen on the horizon.

    Fourvière is known in Lyon as the hill that prays, for its many churches, convents and chapels; the Croix-Rousse, across the rivers, is called the hill that works. Directly below, the old quarter of Vieux-Lyon follows the curve of the Saône. Two funicular railways climb this steep slope, among the oldest still running anywhere. Eastward, the view runs over the whole city to the plains beyond the Rhône.
