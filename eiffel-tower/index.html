    The Eiffel Tower from the Trocadéro

    This part of the route looks at The Eiffel Tower from the Trocadéro.

    Free sample: stop 1

    Read along

    The Eiffel Tower, seen from this terrace, rises across the Seine in one sweep of iron lace.

    It was built for the World's Fair of 1889, which marked one hundred years since the French Revolution. Gustave Eiffel's company won the competition; the first design came from two of his engineers, Maurice Koechlin and Émile Nouguier, refined by the architect Stephen Sauvestre.

    At about three hundred metres, it was the tallest structure built by people anywhere until 1930. With the antennas added since, it now reaches about three hundred and thirty metres.

    The terrace you stand on belongs to the Palais de Chaillot, built for the World's Fair of 1937 by the architects Boileau, Carlu and Azéma. It replaced the older Palais du Trocadéro of 1878: its two curved wings were kept and wrapped in new stone, while the central hall and its towers were pulled down to leave this open esplanade, lined up on the tower and the École Militaire beyond. Napoleon had once planned a palace on this hill for his infant son, the King of Rome, but the project died with his empire.
