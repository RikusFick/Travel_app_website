    This colonnaded courtyard is the forecourt of the Süleymaniye Mosque, built for Sultan Süleyman , Kanuni, the Lawgiver , by his chief architect Sinan. One account dates the mosque between fifteen fifty-one and fifteen fifty-eight; another places the foundation in fifteen fifty and the Friday opening in fifteen fifty-seven.

    Sinan himself called this mosque his journeyman’s work , his kalfalık. Look at the arcades: twenty-eight small domes rest on twenty-four columns , twelve of granite, ten of marble, and two of porphyry , around a three-gated court whose floor is paved in marble.

    At the four corners rise four minarets. Two carry three balconies each and two carry two , ten balconies in all. Tradition links the ten balconies to Süleyman as the tenth Ottoman sultan, and the four minarets to his place as the fourth sultan after the conquest of Istanbul.

    Above the doorway into the prayer hall is the foundation inscription. The mosque’s calligraphy is credited chiefly to the master Ahmed Karahisari and his pupil Hasan Çelebi.
