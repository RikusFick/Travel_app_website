    Read along

    You're on Mount Nemrut, more than two thousand metres up , museum accounts put the summit about two thousand one hundred and fifty metres , on one of the highest peaks of the Eastern Taurus. That pointed hill on the summit isn't natural. It's a huge artificial mound of crushed stone: about a hundred and forty-five metres across and fifty metres high. It was once even higher; UNESCO notes an estimated original height of about sixty metres.

    It was built by a king who wanted to live among the gods. His name was Antiochos the First, and he ruled the small kingdom of Commagene in the first century BC , UNESCO dates his reign about sixty-nine to thirty-four BC , between the powers of Rome in the west and Parthia in the east.

    He called this place his , a temple-tomb and house of the gods. Around the mound he built three terraces, and filled them with colossal statues.
