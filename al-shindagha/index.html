  1. 1

    The point of the Creek

    On the Shindagha waterfront promenade, facing the Creek. This is stop 1 on the route.

    Read along

    You are standing on the spit of land at the mouth of Dubai Creek, where the inlet meets the open Gulf. This is Al Shindagha. From the mid nineteenth century until the 1970s it was one of the most important neighbourhoods of Dubai: the home of the ruling family, of merchants and of pearling crews, with the sea on one side and the Creek harbour on the other.

    The position explains everything. Boats coming in from the Gulf had to pass this point, and anyone living here could watch every dhow that entered or left. Pearling boats set out from the Creek each season, and trading dhows sailed to India, Iran and East Africa and back.

    Look at the houses behind you. They were built side by side from the materials the coast and its trade could offer: coral stone cut from the sea, gypsum plaster, teak and sandalwood brought in by dhow, and palm trunks and fronds. Narrow alleys and small squares run between them, so that the walls shade each other and the lanes stay cool. The tall square towers on the roofs are wind towers, called barjeel, which pulled the breeze down into the rooms.

    By the late twentieth century many of these houses had fallen into ruin. Dubai Municipality restored and rebuilt well over a hundred of them, and the neighbourhood as a whole counts more than 160 historic houses. Eighty of them now form Al Shindagha Museum, which opened in March 2023 and is described as the largest heritage museum in the United Arab Emirates. It is divided into twenty-two pavilions, each on a different side of life in old Dubai: governance and faith, crafts, perfume and jewellery, the sea and the land. The first two pavilions, the Perfume House and the Creek house, had already opened in 2019.

    Look at the skyline across the water and then back at these low sand-coloured walls. Within a single lifetime, Dubai went from this to that, and Shindagha is where the city keeps the memory of what came first.
