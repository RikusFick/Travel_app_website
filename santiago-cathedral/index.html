    This great Baroque front of granite towers is the Obradoiro façade of Santiago de Compostela Cathedral, and the square before it marks the traditional end of the Way of St James, the Camino de Santiago. Pilgrims have walked here from all over Europe since the early Middle Ages to reach the tomb believed to hold St James the Great, one of the twelve apostles. In 1985 UNESCO listed the old town around it as a World Heritage Site.

    According to legend, around the year 814 a hermit called Pelagius saw strange lights in the night sky over the Libredón forest. Bishop Theodomir of Iria declared the tomb found there a miracle, and King Alfonso II of Asturias ordered a chapel built over it. Tradition remembers the king as the first pilgrim.

    The façade itself is a screen. Fernando de Casas Novoa designed it in the eighteenth century, completing it in 1740, to shelter the much older Romanesque church behind it, begun in 1075. Its tall windows light the medieval portal inside. At the top stands St James dressed as a pilgrim, with his disciples Athanasius and Theodore below, and between them the urn of the tomb and the star of the hermit's vision. The two towers, the Bell Tower and the Carraca Tower, rise to between 75 and 80 metres.

    This façade appears on the back of Spain's 1, 2 and 5 cent euro coins, and the square's name, Obradoiro, is Galician for "workshop", after the stonemasons who worked here while the cathedral was built.
