    Read along

    The cathedral façade rises in front of you in pink sandstone from the Vosges, topped by a single spire on the north side.

    The spire is about one hundred and forty-two metres high. From 1647 to 1874 it made Strasbourg Cathedral the tallest building in the world, and it remains the tallest structure surviving from the Middle Ages. The second spire was never built, which gives the cathedral its unmistakable lopsided outline.

    The façade was begun in 1277. Its first named master builder, Erwin, known as Erwin of Steinbach, led the work from 1284 to 1318 and designed the lower levels, the three portals and the great rose. The stone is a pink sandstone quarried in the hills west of the city, and the spire itself was finished in 1439 by Johannes Hültz of Cologne.

    The façade rises in three levels, divided by four slender buttresses: the portals at ground level, then the rose window, and at the top a balustrade. Each of the three portals has its own theme: the childhood of Christ on the left, redemption in the centre and the Last Judgement on the right. The sculpture stands forward from the wall behind a network of slender columns, spires and arches, like an outer screen of stone.

    During the Revolution hundreds of statues on the façade were destroyed, and nineteenth-century sculptors carved replacements for those that could not be saved.
