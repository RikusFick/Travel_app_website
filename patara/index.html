    Among the tombs of Patara’s necropolis, Markia’s monumental tomb ranks as one of the most important, and its excavation has been finished. It occupies a site in the southeast of Patara’s bay. Builders set the eastern façade onto the slope and the rock, and they turned the western façade toward the harbour.

    Inscriptions indicate that, when the tomb was built, it stood in a wooded area. It was placed to be visible from the harbour side; in that way it joined the prospect and the landscape around it. The harbour view was clearly treated as an important factor when this site was chosen.

    Eight steps lead up the high podium that carries the tomb. The corners follow the Doric order, and the columns of the façade follow the Corinthian. The upper part is finished with three fasciae, then a convex frieze, then a triangular pediment.

    The burial chamber is square, with a vaulted ceiling, and it has a bench shaped like the Greek letter gamma that held two sarcophagi. A single lower chamber, called the hyposorion, is vaulted, with access from the northern side of the podium.

    The inscriptions record that one of the sarcophagi was given to Markia, together with Alkimos her husband, and that the other was given to her father and her brother.
