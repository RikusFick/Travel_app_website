    This long, low building shaped like a giant eye, floating on its pool, is L'Hemisfèric, the first building of Valencia's City of Arts and Sciences.

    The whole complex stands in a river that is no longer there. After a catastrophic flood in 1957, the river Turia was diverted around the city, and its old bed was turned into a long sunken park. The City of Arts and Sciences fills its south-eastern end.

    The idea came in 1989, when the president of the Valencian government, Joan Lerma, visited the new science city in Paris. The first plan, drawn by the Valencian architect and engineer Santiago Calatrava, had three parts: a science museum, a planetarium and a communications tower more than 370 metres high, which would have been one of the tallest in the world. Opponents called it a "work of the pharaohs". After a change of government in 1996, the tower was dropped, an opera house and an aquarium were added, and the project took its present name.

    L'Hemisfèric opened in April 1998. Calatrava's eye is about 110 metres long, built of concrete, with an eyelid of long aluminium slats that fold up to open it. The dome inside, the iris, holds the planetarium and a giant-screen cinema. The bottom of the pool is glass, so the reflection completes the eye. Calatrava said that because the site is so dry and so close to the sea, he made water the mirror for all the architecture.
