    The top of Cappadocia

    This part of the route looks at The top of Cappadocia.

    Free sample: stop 1

    Play0:00 / –:––
    Read along

    You are standing on the summit of Uçhisar Castle, the highest point in this part of Cappadocia. The town sits about five kilometres from Nevşehir, in the heart of the Cappadocian volcanic plateau. The ministry calls this rock Cappadocia's largest and most striking fairy chimney, visible from across the region, and says its summit is the one place for a bird's-eye view of the valleys as a whole.

    This is not a castle built stone by stone. It is two joined pointed cones of soft volcanic rock, hollowed by hand over many centuries. Locals call the larger peak the Agha's Castle and the smaller the Sergeant's Castle , Ağanın Kalesi and Çavuşun Kalesi. The ministry measures the mass as more than fifty metres high on the south side and more than a hundred on the north.

    Since at least Roman times people hollowed it into rooms, houses, refuges, stores, cisterns, tombs and cellars, linked by stairs and tunnels. Large stone balls , gülle , were kept on the rock for defence. It served as both lookout and stronghold against Arab raids, and it kept that importance through the Seljuk and Beylik centuries of the twelfth to fourteenth centuries.

    Its very name, Uçhisar , frontier castle , comes from its role on the border under the Seljuks and the Karamanids. In that age the ministry groups it with Başhisar at Ürgüp and with Ortahisar as three high lookouts that guarded the Nevşehir country.

    On the summit, the ministry notes carved jars, rock tombs and a large cistern. From here you can see both volcanoes that built this landscape: Erciyes to the east and Hasan to the south-west , ministry accounts say this is the one summit where both appear together. People lived in and around the rock into the 1960s; after that the castle became a visited ruin rather than a village home.
