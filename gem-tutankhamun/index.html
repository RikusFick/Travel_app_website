    Tutankhamun and his family

    In the Tutankhamun Galleries, at the opening area that introduces the king's identity and family (the gallery layout may change; follow the theme panels). This is stop 1 on the route.

    Play0:00 / –:––
    Read along

    Before the gold, meet the boy. The museum dates his birth to around 1345 BC, and his reign to roughly 1336 to 1327 BC. His birth name was Tutankhaten, which means "living image of Aten".

    Aten was the sun disk worshipped by Akhenaten, the pharaoh who ruled when the boy was born. Akhenaten had turned away from Egypt's traditional religion and built a new capital in the desert at Amarna.

    Scholars have long argued about who his parents were. DNA studies identified his father as the mummy found in tomb KV55, which many scholars think is Akhenaten. They identified his mother as a woman known only as the Younger Lady, whose mummy was found in tomb KV35. Some scholars question the reliability of such ancient DNA results, and they propose other family trees.

    Tutankhaten came to the throne at the age of eight or nine. As a child he was engaged to Ankhesenpaaten, a daughter of Akhenaten and Nefertiti, and his half-sister. Later the pair took new names, Tutankhamun and Ankhesenamun: in both, the Aten gave way to Amun, the great god of Thebes.

    The court left Amarna. The king and queen lived at the traditional capitals of Thebes and Memphis. A stela from his reign, known as the Restoration Stela, describes a country in chaos and the king's restoration of the temples.

    He died young, after a reign of about nine years. Ay, an old official, buried him and became king after him, and then the general Horemheb. The cause of his death has never been settled, and the museum's brochure lists theories about his health and death among its themes.
