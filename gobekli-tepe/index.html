    This hill is Göbekli Tepe. It stands about fifteen kilometres north-east of Şanlıurfa, on high ground near the village of Örencik , not beside a river or in a valley, but on an elevation that looks out over the Harran Plain.

    In 1963, Turkish and American researchers surveying the region noticed limestone blocks on the hillside. Because of the site's high position and strange topography, its importance was not fully appreciated at first. The blocks were taken for a cemetery. Continuous excavations ran from 1995 to 2006 under the Şanlıurfa Museum Directorate, with the German Archaeological Institute , first Professor Harald Hauptmann, later Dr Klaus Schmidt. After Schmidt's death in 2014, an international team continued the work. The uppermost layer is later surface fill where farming took place; the important layers beneath belong to the Pre-Pottery Neolithic.

    The great stone enclosures here belong to the Pre-Pottery Neolithic, between about 9600 and 8200 BC , more than eleven thousand years ago , built by hunter-gatherers who did not yet farm and did not yet use pottery. UNESCO places the site in Upper Mesopotamia, the region that saw the emergence of the most ancient farming communities in the world. The builders stood on the threshold of that change. Göbekli Tepe became a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2018.
