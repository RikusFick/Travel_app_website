    Read along

    You are standing in the Sebasteion, between its two rows of carved marble façades. Aphrodisias was the city of Aphrodite, goddess of love and beauty. Its wealth came from the marble quarries in the hills nearby and from the skill of its sculptors.

    This building, the Sebasteion, was dedicated to the Roman emperors, who were worshipped as gods. It was built over two generations, from about AD 20 to 60, under the emperors Tiberius to Nero, and paid for by leading families of the city. Two three-storey buildings, about twelve metres high, face each other across a processional way about fourteen metres wide, with columns in the Doric, Ionic and Corinthian styles, one order above the other. At the east end stood a temple. Relief panels filled the upper two storeys along their whole length , some two hundred of them , so the façades looked like walls of pictures. The panels showed emperors, gods, Greek myths and the peoples of Rome's empire, and more than eighty of them survive. A famous panel shows Emperor Claudius overpowering a female figure labelled Britannia: Rome's conquest of Britain, carved in marble here in Asia Minor.
