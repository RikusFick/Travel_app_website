    Read along

    You are at the Gymnasium, the great training ground of the sanctuary of Zeus at Olympia. The sanctuary lies in the valley where the Alpheios and Kladeos rivers meet, and the Olympic Games were held here from 776 BC to AD 393.

    The Gymnasium was built in stages in the Hellenistic period, starting with its south portico in the early second century BC. Four porticoes surrounded an open courtyard, in a complex about two hundred and twenty metres long and one hundred and twenty metres wide. This was where the runners and the pentathletes trained, as the travel writer Pausanias also noted when he came here in the second century AD.

    The east portico was about two hundred and twelve metres long, almost exactly the length of the racetrack in the stadium. It held the xystos, a roofed track for training when the weather was harsh, and beside it ran an open-air track called the paradromis. The west portico was probably washed away by floods of the Kladeos in the fourth century AD; it may have had two storeys and housed athletes.

    Near the south-east corner, opposite the entrance to the sacred grove, stood an elegant gateway with eighteen Corinthian columns. Between 2013 and 2015 the Ephorate of Antiquities of Ilia excavated the east portico, the first dig by the Greek Archaeological Service in the core of the site.
