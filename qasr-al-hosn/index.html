  1. 1

    The white fort

    In the open plaza facing the fort's white walls. This is stop 1 on the route.

    Play0:00 / –:––
    Read along

    In front of you is the oldest stone building in Abu Dhabi, standing among the towers of the modern city. Qasr Al Hosn means "the palace fort", and for almost two centuries it was the home of the ruling family and the seat of government.

    What you are looking at is really several buildings from different centuries, one inside the other. At the heart is a single watchtower. Around it grew a small inner fort with four towers. And wrapped around that is the long, high wall of the Outer Palace, built between 1939 and the mid-1940s. When the Outer Palace went up it was the largest building on the island, rising over a town of palm-frond houses and a few thousand people.

    Think about what builders had to work with on a flat, sandy island with almost no trees. They used what the sea gave them. The walls are coral stone and sea stone, cut from the shallows. They are bonded and plastered with a lime made by burning coral and shells, mixed with sand and water. People here called this plaster juss bahari, "sea plaster". Thick walls of this kind kept the rooms behind them noticeably cooler than the air outside.

    The brilliant white you see is not how the fort first looked. The plaster would once have taken the warmer colour of the local lime and sand. The walls were painted white during works in the late 1970s and early 1980s, and that white has become the fort's public face.

    Look up at the towers on the corners and the narrow openings near their tops. Those were for watching and for defence, not for light.

    The open plaza around you is also new. It was once filled by the town itself: houses of palm fronds, the market and lanes running down towards the shore, all within sight of the fort. Early photographs from 1901 and 1904 show the fort standing almost alone on the sand, with the sea close by. When the city was rebuilt in the 1960s and 1970s, everything around it was cleared, and the fort was left as the one surviving link to the old settlement.
