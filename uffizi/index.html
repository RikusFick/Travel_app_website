Fifteen carefully sequenced stops lead through the Uffizi's top rooms from thirteenth-century altarpieces to Baroque drama. Works offline inside the gallery so you can move room by room without losing track of key paintings.

Stops

  1. 1

    Offices for a duke

    Start in the Uffizi courtyard to see how Giorgio Vasari designed these twin wings as Medici administrative offices in 1560.

  2. 2

    Three Madonnas: painting changes direction

    Compare three monumental Maestà altarpieces by Cimabue, Duccio, and Giotto to watch flat gold icons turn three-dimensional.

  3. 3

    The Duke and Duchess of Urbino

    Examine Piero della Francesca's paired profile portraits of the Duke and Duchess of Urbino set against a sweeping Tuscan landscape.

  4. 4

    Botticelli's Primavera

    Study Botticelli's Primavera, an enigmatic mythological allegory painted for a Medici townhouse around 1480.

  5. 5

    The Birth of Venus

    Pause before Botticelli's Birth of Venus, painted on canvas with gold leaf highlights for the Medici family.

  6. 6

    The Tribune: a jewel box

    Peek into Buontalenti's octagonal Tribune, capped by a dome inlaid with nearly six thousand Indian Ocean mother-of-pearl shells.

  7. 7

    Leonardo's unfinished Adoration of the Magi

    Examine Leonardo da Vinci's unfinished Adoration of the Magi, revealing his swirling compositional underdrawing in brown and ochre.

  8. 8

    Young Leonardo's Annunciation

    See young Leonardo's Annunciation, painted in Verrocchio's workshop when the artist was around twenty years old.

  9. 9

    The view over the Arno

    Catch your breath at the south corridor windows overlooking the Ponte Vecchio and Vasari Corridor across the Arno.

  10. 10

    Michelangelo's Doni Tondo

    Look closely at Michelangelo's Doni Tondo, his only surviving finished panel painting, framed by carved wooden heads.

  11. 11

    Raphael's Madonna of the Goldfinch

    Study Raphael's tender Madonna of the Goldfinch, painted during his Florentine years and painstakingly restored after a collapse.

  12. 12

    Titian's Venus of Urbino

    Visit Titian's 1538 Venus of Urbino to contrast Venetian warm oil colour against Florentine crisp line.

  13. 13

    Caravaggio's Bacchus

    Look at Caravaggio's youthful Bacchus reclining with a glass of wine, painted with startling naturalism in Rome.

  14. 14

    Artemisia Gentileschi's Judith

    Pause before Artemisia Gentileschi's fierce Judith Beheading Holofernes, a landmark of seventeenth-century dramatic chiaroscuro.

  15. 15

    Caravaggio's Medusa

    Finish at Caravaggio's painted wooden parade shield of Medusa, frozen in mid-scream with writhing snakes.
