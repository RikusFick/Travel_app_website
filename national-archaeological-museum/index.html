    The Mask of Agamemnon

    In Room 4, the Mycenaean gallery, at the gold death masks in the central cases. (Indoors: manual play.). This is stop 1 on the route.

    Free sample: stop 1

    Play0:00 / –:––
    Read along

    This gold face is the so-called Mask of Agamemnon. Greece's Ministry of Culture calls it the best known of Schliemann's finds from the royal tombs at Mycenae.

    The excavation of Grave Circle A in 1876 was carried out by Heinrich Schliemann. A Greek official, the Ephor of Antiquities Panagiotis Stamatakis, supervised it.

    The mask was hammered from a thick sheet of gold against a wooden background, and the details were then chased on with a sharp tool. Two holes near the ears held it over the dead man's face with twine.

    Five gold masks were found in the circle, probably made for rulers. This is the only one showing a bearded man.

    Schliemann first identified the face with Agamemnon, the Homeric king who led the expedition to Troy.

    But the dead buried there belonged to the first generations of Mycenaean rulers, in the sixteenth century BC. That is at least four centuries before the war archaeologically identified with the Trojan War.

    Stamatakis recorded the six tombs of the circle with careful method. The museum says his diary put an end to several of Schliemann's exaggerations, and restored the scientific credibility of the excavation.
