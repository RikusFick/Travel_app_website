    The Clérigos Church has a narrow, richly carved Baroque front, above a double stairway.

    It was built for the Brotherhood of the Clérigos, a brotherhood of clergy. It was formed in 1707, when three older brotherhoods of Porto joined together, and it helped poor priests in sickness, poverty and death. For years it met in the Misericórdia church, and in December 1731 it approved plans for a church of its own, by the Italian architect Nicolau Nasoni. Nasoni was born in Tuscany in 1691, trained as a painter, and came to Porto in 1725 after working in Malta. His first recorded work there was painting in the cathedral, where he introduced quadratura, a way of painting architecture in illusionist perspective so that flat walls seem to open into space. Work began in April 1732, on land the brotherhood had been given in the Campo do Olival, then the largest churchyard in the city.

    The site was long and steep, so Nasoni fitted a chain of buildings onto it: the church at one end, the tower at the other, and the brotherhood's house between them. The front is narrow and tall, which suited the site.

    It is perhaps the most Baroque part of the whole complex: straight and curved lines played against each other, with garlands, scrolls, urns and flaming torches carved in granite. At the top of the gable, a three-armed papal cross rises above a medallion with the monogram of Mary, an A and an M interlaced.

    The work on these buildings lasted more than thirty years. Nasoni also designed the façade of the Misericórdia church, where the brotherhood had first met, and he joined the brotherhood himself in 1743. He died in Porto in 1773, aged 82, looked after by the brotherhood as a poor man, and he was buried in this church. He is believed to lie beneath the high altar.
