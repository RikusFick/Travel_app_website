  1. 1

    The Palace of the Nation

    On the forecourt, facing the main façade of the palace. This is stop 1 on the route.

    Read along

    Qasr Al Watan means "the Palace of the Nation". In front of you is the presidential palace of the United Arab Emirates, on the Ras Al Akhdar headland at the western tip of Abu Dhabi island, beside the Emirates Palace hotel and inside the wider Presidential Palace compound.

    This is not a home. Nobody lives here. It is a working building of state. The compound around it holds the formal offices of the President, the Vice President and the Crown Prince of Abu Dhabi, and this palace is where foreign heads of state are received and where the federal cabinet and the Supreme Council, the body made up of the rulers of the seven emirates, meet. Leaders including the Chinese president Xi Jinping and Pope Francis have been received here.

    The building took several years to raise. Construction began around 2010; the main contractor, Consolidated Contractors Company, handed it over in 2015, and the finishing work ran on until 2017. For its first two years it served only official business. In March 2019 it opened to the public for the first time, and even now parts of it close on some days so that the business of government can go on.

    Look at the façade. It is faced in white granite and limestone, chosen to last for centuries and to reflect the sun's heat rather than soak it up. White was picked as a colour of purity and peace, and it also echoes the pale buildings of the old coastal towns of the Gulf. Look up at the domes: their gilded bands carry flowing Arabesque patterns. The style borrows from many periods of Islamic building, with hints of the Mughal architecture of India in its large domes, its courtyards and its gardens, the same family of forms you see at the Sheikh Zayed Grand Mosque.

    As you walk in, notice the arched colonnade along the front. Shaded arcades like this are a feature of traditional Gulf houses, built to give shelter from the heat. Then look at the great doors. The palace's doors are solid maple, chosen for its pale colour and its strength, and carved by hand with details picked out in 23-carat gold. Each one is reported to have taken about 350 hours of work.
