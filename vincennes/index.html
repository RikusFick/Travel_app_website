    The keep of Vincennes is about fifty metres high, the tallest medieval keep in Europe.

    Kings came here long before the tower existed. In the twelfth century Louis VII built a hunting manor at the edge of this great wood, and in the thirteenth century Saint Louis held council here, and was remembered for rendering justice under an oak in Vincennes. When Saint Louis brought the Crown of Thorns to France, he escorted the relic through Vincennes on its way to Paris, and a few thorns and a fragment of the True Cross were kept here. It was also at Vincennes that he took leave of his family before setting out on the crusade from which he never returned. The manor saw royal life and royal death: Philip III married here in 1274, and Louis X died here in 1316, and Charles IV in 1328. Charles V himself was born here in 1338. The tower itself was a reaction to fear. In 1358 a Paris uprising stormed the royal palace where the future Charles V lived. A few years later work on the keep began, on foundations laid by Philip VI, and the tower rose in only about ten years.

    The keep is a square tower about sixteen metres across, with walls more than three metres thick and a round turret at each corner. Each floor is one great vaulted room resting on a slender central column. Hidden in the masonry is an iron frame of about twelve tonnes, tie-bars and clamps that hold the walls together and allowed large windows. Between 1372 and 1380 Charles V then enclosed the whole site in a wall more than a kilometre long, with nine towers about forty metres high, big enough to shelter a small town.
