  1. 1

    The Red River

    On or beside the old stone bridge over the Kızılırmak, in the centre of Avanos. This is stop 1 on the route.

    Play0:00 / –:––
    Read along

    Welcome to Avanos. Below this old stone bridge flows the Kızılırmak , the Red River. Official culture wording calls it Turkey’s longest river. In ancient times this town was called Venessa, in the wording of both ministry and district pages.

    Its name comes from the reddish earth and silt the river once carried. Official culture wording says that in earlier times the water took on a matching red colour as it watered the tuff and clay soils here , and that after dams were built upstream that tint largely disappeared. Look at the banks: the same soft red clay feeds the potters’ workshops a short walk away.

    Stand and listen to the water. Avanos grew here because the river brought clay as well as a crossing. The same soft red clay still waits on these banks for the wheel.

  2. 2

    The potters of Avanos

    In the potters’ streets of the town centre, among the workshop doors. This is stop 2 on the route.

    Play0:00 / –:––
    Read along

    You are among the işlik , the workshops , of Avanos. Official ministry and district accounts says pottery on the wheel here is said to go back to the Hittites, and that the craft passed from people to people, father to son, down to the present.

    The clay comes from the hills around Avanos and from the old beds of the Kızılırmak. Potters sift the soft, oily earth, knead it with water into a workable paste, and shape it on a kick wheel , a çark turned with the foot. Finished pots dry first in the sun, then in the shade, before firing.

    Traditional products named on the official pages include cooking pots, water jugs, and storage jars for winter food. If a workshop invites you, you may try a turn at the wheel , leave the finished pieces to the potter.

    District pages also pass on local stories: that caravans once sold pots along the Kayseri–Gülşehir–Aksaray roads, bartering a pot for its fill of rice or cheese, and that a man who could not throw pots found it hard to marry. Treat those as local sayings, not dated history.

    Look at the clay under your fingernails if you try the wheel , that is the Red River’s gift, worked in the town centre.

  3. 3

    Sarıhan caravanserai (optional)

    At Sarıhan, the Seljuk caravanserai a few kilometres south-east of Avanos. This is stop 3 on the route.

    Play0:00 / –:––
    Read along

    If you have driven out this far, you stand at Sarıhan , the Yellow Han , on the old Aksaray–Kayseri caravan road. Official ministry inventory wording places it about five kilometres south-east of Avanos and dates the Seljuk building to twelve forty-nine, in the time of Sultan İzzettin Keykavus the Second.

    The plan has two parts: an open courtyard and a covered hall. A tall portal with rows of mukarnas , stalactite carving , leads into the court. The inventory notes a small mosque reached by stairs on the upper level of the entrance wing. The building belongs to the Directorate General of Foundations; official inventory wording notes a restoration for visitors.

    Stand in the courtyard. Merchants and animals once rested here on the caravan road between the river country and the high roads east. The open court and the covered hall mark that pause in the journey.
