    Read along

    You're looking at Santa Maria delle Grazie, home of one of the most famous paintings in the world: Leonardo da Vinci's Last Supper.

    This church and its Dominican convent were begun under the Sforza family, the dukes of Milan. In the 1490s, Duke Ludovico Sforza decided to turn the church into his family's burial place, and rebuilt its east end with the elegant dome you can see. The design is often attributed to the architect Bramante, though not everyone agrees.

    The two halves of the building are two different ideas. The long nave, begun in 1463 and attributed to the architect Guiniforte Solari, still has pointed Gothic arches inside. The new east end, finished in the late 1490s, is a Renaissance cube about twenty metres on each side, as wide as all three aisles together, crowned by a hemispherical dome on a tall drum. Outside, both are clad in brick, and the east end is trimmed with Lombard terracotta. Even the numbers inside were symbolic: sixteen round windows in the dome, and groups of twelve discs, the number of the apostles.

    Ludovico was also Leonardo's patron. As part of this project, he commissioned a painting for the end wall of the convent's dining hall, the refectory, next to the church.

    Ludovico's young wife, Beatrice d'Este, was buried in this church in 1497, while Leonardo was still at work on the painting.
