    The National Coach Museum keeps most of its collection of royal coaches in this modern building.

    It opened here on 23 May 2015, one hundred and ten years after the museum was founded on that same date in 1905. The design is by the Brazilian architect Paulo Mendes da Rocha, winner of the Pritzker Prize in 2006, working with the Portuguese architect Ricardo Bak Gordon and the engineer Rui Furtado. The site was once the army's General Workshops in Belém, bought by the state in 1994 so that the museum could finally have room.

    The main exhibition hall is a long box raised off the ground on slender round pillars, with a smaller annex beside it for offices, an auditorium and a restaurant, joined to it by a high walkway. Mendes da Rocha said that the museum has no door and opens to every side. The space under the hall is meant as a public square, part of the city rather than shut away from it.

    The museum counts more than seventy historic vehicles. Besides them, the collection includes harness, uniforms of the royal household, silver trumpets of the royal band, and portraits of the kings and queens of the House of Braganza. Most of the coaches belonged to the Portuguese royal house, from the sixteenth to the nineteenth century. Later, vehicles from the Church and from private collections joined them, so that the collection traces the whole history of horse-drawn transport at the courts of Europe, up to the arrival of the motor car.
