    Read along

    The Porte Narbonnaise is the grand eastern gate of the Cité, flanked by two massive towers built in the thirteenth century under the kings of France.

    People lived on this hill long before the walls. Archaeologists found traces of settlement from about 800 BC nearby, and the Romans took the town in 122 BC. It stood on the road from Narbonne to Bordeaux, which is where this gate takes its name.

    The gate replaced a vanished castle that once guarded the main way into the town. Look at the two towers: each carries a pointed beak to turn aside missiles, and the passage was closed by a double portcullis. One tower held a store for provisions and the other a water cistern, so that the defenders could hold out through a long siege. A statue of the Virgin stands in a niche above, while the drawbridge in front was added by Viollet-le-Duc and never existed in the Middle Ages.
