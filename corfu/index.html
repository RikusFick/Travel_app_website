    Read along

    The Spianada, Corfu Town's great square, stretches between the old town and the Old Fortress. Its name comes from the Venetian spianata, a broad, open space.

    The emptiness was deliberate. The ground was first cleared here in 1516, the World Heritage evaluation says, and after the Ottoman siege of 1537 the strip was made wider by knocking down the houses that faced the citadel walls.

    Venice wanted a flat field in front of the fortress, as long as a musket ball could fly. Greece's heritage authorities count about 2,000 buildings pulled down to open this field of fire for the guns.

    After the French came the British, and they made a cricket ground of the open space east of the Liston.

    Near the southern end stands a round temple ringed by Ionic columns, dedicated to Sir Thomas Maitland, Britain's High Commissioner.

    It is known as the Maitland Rotunda, and the architectural historian David Boswell explains that it was meant to be useful rather than a memorial: it sheltered the biggest Venetian cistern on the square.

    A broad stone terrace around it collected clean rainwater. The World Heritage evaluation names George Whitmore as its architect. Boswell fits the work into a single year, 1821.
