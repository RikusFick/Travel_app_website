    The Royal Building of Mafra stretches across the square, a palace, a basilica and a convent joined in one vast Baroque façade.

    Its story begins with a vow. According to the official histories, King João the Fifth promised a Franciscan convent at Mafra if his queen gave him an heir, and in 1711 he authorised it. The first plan was modest, a friary for thirteen friars. Then the plans grew, first to eighty friars and, in the early 1720s, to three hundred, and the king made the project a royal palace as well, a monument meant to set Portugal beside the great powers of Europe. The first stone was laid in 1717, and workers came from every part of the kingdom.

    The design is credited to a German goldsmith turned architect, Johann Friedrich Ludwig, known in Portugal as Ludovice. The front is more than two hundred metres long, with a great square tower at each end and the basilica in the middle, its façade lined with statues of four saints, among them Saint Francis and Saint Clare. The palace counts about 1,200 rooms inside, and the convent lies behind.

    The building's construction is the subject of the novel Baltasar and Blimunda, by the Nobel laureate José Saramago. In 2019 the Royal Building of Mafra, with its garden and hunting park, was listed by UNESCO as a World Heritage Site.
