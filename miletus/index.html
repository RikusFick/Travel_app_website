    The theatre

    Free sample: stop 1

    Read along

    This is the theatre of Miletus, one of the largest theatres in Asia Minor.

    It began much smaller. The first theatre here was probably built around the fourth century BC and held a few thousand people. Its builders chose a natural hollow in the slope, a shape that suited a Greek theatre. That is why the theatre does not follow the regular street grid of Miletus. Under the emperor Nero, a new stage front was added, divided by columns, niches and cornices. The Romans rebuilt it on a huge scale, with seats for about fifteen thousand and a richly decorated marble stage front. Two vaulted passages, one on each side, led into the seating and to a massive cellar with chambers inside it. The topmost tier of seats was later taken down.

    A Greek inscription on the fifth row of seats has been translated as “the place of the Jews who are also God-worshipers.”

    The ancient audience looked out towards the sea, with a harbour below the theatre.
