    You are at the entrance to Kaymaklı Underground City. Look at the houses around you. They are ordinary village homes. The town stands about twenty kilometres south of Nevşehir, on the Niğde–Nevşehir road, and about ten kilometres from Derinkuyu Underground City.

    The ministry gives the village's ancient name as Enegüp. Its people built their homes around roughly a hundred tunnels of the underground city. Some of those tunnels open into courtyards, and villagers still pass through them to use suitable spaces below as cellars, storerooms and stables.

    Cappadocia holds somewhere between 150 and 200 underground settlements of varying sizes, most of them carved downwards into soft tuff. The ministry says they were built first for security. Its brochure describes underground cities made up of hundreds of rooms, linked by long galleries and labyrinth-like tunnels. Underground it also stays cool in summer and warm in winter.

    UNESCO notes that, to resist Arab invasions, Christian communities in Cappadocia banded together into subterranean towns such as Kaymaklı and Derinkuyu, which served as places of refuge.

    Ministry and tourism-encyclopedia accounts describe this complex as having eight levels in all. Four levels have been unearthed, with spaces clustered around the ventilation shafts. The underground city opened to visitors in 1964, and today its four open levels are lit.

    Kaymaklı belongs to the UNESCO World Heritage Site "Göreme National Park and the Rock Sites of Cappadocia", inscribed in 1985.
