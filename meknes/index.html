    The Bou Inania Madrasa

    In the courtyard of the madrasa, in the medina near the Grand Mosque. This is stop 1 on the route.

    Free sample: stop 1

    Read along

    Meknes is a city of two halves: an old medina, and beside it the vast imperial city of a seventeenth-century sultan. This madrasa stands in the older half. It was a college where religious students lived while they studied the Islamic sciences.

    A heritage catalogue counts it among the masterpieces of the Marinid sultan Abu al-Hasan. Two inscriptions inside date it to 1336. One is a poem around the prayer niche of the prayer hall.

    The other runs along the wooden lintels of this courtyard, in praise of our master Abu al-Hasan, emir of the faithful. The building was first called the New Madrasa. His son, the sultan Abu Inan, restored it and renamed it after himself.

    The Marinids gave Meknes three madrasas in all, as both the World Heritage evaluation and a heritage itinerary record.

    The plan is simple. A long hallway runs from the street door to this courtyard. That door is wooden, sheathed in finely worked copper. At the centre of the courtyard sits a marble basin shaped like a shell.

    Wooden lattice screens between the pillars once divided the courtyard from the galleries that lead to the ground-floor student rooms. The upper rooms look down through decorated windows. The dome over the entrance came later, in the reign of Moulay Ismail.

    Look at the decoration rising up the walls; the catalogue sees the mark of al-Andalus in every part of it. If the roof terrace is open, go up for a wide view over the medina.
