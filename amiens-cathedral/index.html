    The west front of Amiens Cathedral is crowded with carved figures, one of the finest Gothic sculpture programmes in Europe.

    The old cathedral burned in 1218. The architect Robert de Luzarches proposed something enormous, and Bishop Évrard de Fouilloy laid the first stone in 1220. Much of it was paid for by the blue dye trade: Amiens grew rich on woad, a plant that gives a royal blue, and you can find woad merchants carved on the south side of the nave. The great rose window above was replaced in the sixteenth century in the Flamboyant style.

    The front is about forty-nine metres wide, and its two towers are not twins: the north tower rises to about sixty-six metres and the south tower to about sixty-one.

    The lower front, with its portals, went up in a single campaign, from 1220 to about 1236, which is why it feels so unified; the upper parts of the towers came much later, in the fourteenth and early fifteenth centuries. Much of what you see, though, passed through the hands of Eugène Viollet-le-Duc, who worked on the cathedral from 1849 to 1874. He wanted to bring back its medieval spirit, added gargoyles and other Gothic details, and largely recreated the gallery joining the two towers, modelling it on Chartres. His work was sometimes controversial. When the front was cleaned in the late twentieth century, specialists found under the grime traces of the original paint: the portals were once brightly coloured, a look that is recreated with light shows in the evenings.

    The central portal is dedicated to the Last Judgement. In the tympanum Christ presides, showing his wounds, with the Virgin Mary and Saint John kneeling to plead for humanity. On the pillar between the doors stands a statue of Christ blessing, known as the Beau Dieu of Amiens.

    The left portal honours Saint Firmin, the first bishop of Amiens, and the right the Virgin. Above them runs the Gallery of Kings, twenty-two life-size statues. Low on the portals, small carved quatrefoils pair the signs of the zodiac with the labours of each month, a farming calendar in stone, remarkably well preserved.
