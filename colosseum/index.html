Walk Rome's largest Roman amphitheatre with eight offline audio stories, starting at the base of Nero's Colossus and ending high on the outer arches. Stories cover how fifty thousand spectators entered, how the arena floor hid cages and lifts, and how the ruin survived the Middle Ages.

Stops

  1. 1

    Where the giant stood

    Stand beside the low brick square near the metro exit where the giant bronze Colossus of Nero once rose and gave the amphitheatre its name.

  2. 2

    Four storeys of stone

    Look up at the surviving north outer wall where Travertine arches rise four storeys high and still hold sockets for the sun-awning masts.

  3. 3

    Constantine's borrowed arch

    Stop by the Arch of Constantine between the arena and the Palatine Hill to trace how fourth-century builders reused sculptures from earlier emperors.

  4. 4

    Getting fifty thousand in

    Step into the vaulted ground-floor corridors to see how numbered arches and radial staircases sorted crowds quickly to their seats.

  5. 5

    A seat for every rank

    Take in the oval bowl from an interior terrace, tracing the social ladder from marble senator boxes down front to wooden benches at the top.

  6. 6

    Under the sand

    Look down into the exposed brick maze of the hypogeum beneath the vanished wooden floor, where trapdoors and winches lifted beasts and stage sets.

  7. 7

    The games

    Hear how a full day at the games unfolded, from morning animal hunts and midday executions to afternoon gladiatorial bouts.

  8. 8

    Quarry, castle, shrine

    Finish along the upper galleries with a look at the arena's later chapters as a medieval fortress, stone quarry, and Christian shrine.
