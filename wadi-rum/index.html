  1. 1

    The Visitor Centre and the Seven Pillars of Wisdom

    On the Visitor Centre terrace, looking across at the long mountain with deep vertical grooves. This is stop 1 on the route.

    Play0:00 / –:––
    Read along

    You are standing at the gateway to the Wadi Rum Protected Area: more than seven hundred square kilometres of sandstone mountains, granite, and valleys of red and yellow sand near the Saudi border. In 2011, UNESCO listed it as a World Heritage Site for both its nature and its culture, a rare double honour.

    The mountain opposite, with its row of rounded columns, is the one everyone photographs. The local Bedouin call it Jabal al-Mazmar. In the 1980s it was given a new name, the Seven Pillars of Wisdom, after the book T. E. Lawrence wrote about the Arab Revolt of the First World War. The pillars in Lawrence's title had nothing to do with this mountain. They come from the Book of Proverbs: "Wisdom has built her house; she has hewn out her seven pillars." Count the columns if you like. You will not find exactly seven.

    The real story here is far older than Lawrence. UNESCO counts some 25,000 rock carvings and 20,000 inscriptions across the area, spanning about twelve thousand years of human life. In 2025, Jordan's antiquities ministry announced one more: carved cartouches of the Egyptian pharaoh Ramesses III, according to reports of the find. Keep your eyes on the rock walls. People have been writing on them for a very long time.
