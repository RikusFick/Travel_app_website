    The Church of San Vicente

    Begin at San Vicente, the oldest surviving sixteenth-century church in the Old Town.

    Play0:00 / –:––
    Read along

    This tall Gothic church of sandstone, with buttresses and round rose windows, is the Church of San Vicente, the oldest building still standing in San Sebastián.

    It replaced an earlier church damaged by fire in 1489. The master masons Miguel de Santa Celay and Juan de Urrutia began it in 1507, and it was finished in 1574. Inside are three naves, and the great main altarpiece, carved by Ambrosio de Bengoechea, is counted among the finest Spanish altarpieces in the style that followed Michelangelo. The organ was built by the famous French maker Aristide Cavaillé-Coll in 1868, and many of the stained-glass windows come from the Maumejean workshop of Biarritz, in the early twentieth century. The church is a main venue of the city's classical music festival, the Musical Fortnight.

    Its age makes it rare here. San Sebastián is an old town: it received its charter from the king of Navarre around 1180, and in 1200 it passed to Castile. Many early settlers were Gascons from Bayonne and beyond, which is why several local place names, Urgull among them, come from Gascon. But in 1813 almost the whole walled town burned. Only a handful of buildings survived, and San Vicente is one of them.
