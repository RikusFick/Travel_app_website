    A few steps ago you were on a busy Marrakech street. The foundation that runs the garden says it covers about nine thousand square metres, a little under one hectare, closed in on every side by high walls.

    Inside, it describes a maze of alleys that cross at different levels, and brightly painted buildings that mix Art Deco and Moorish ideas. It was created over some forty years.

    Jacques Majorelle, a French painter of orientalist scenes, lived from 1886 to 1962.

    His father was the cabinetmaker Louis Majorelle, a leading figure of the Art Nouveau school of Nancy.

    He first came to Morocco in 1917. An exhibition text from the Institut du monde arabe in Paris says he came to recover from a lung illness.

    It adds that Marshal Lyautey, also from Nancy, helped him settle. The foundation says Lyautey, then the French Resident-General, invited him.

    Marrakech won him over. From the 1920s, the institute says, exhibitions made him known as "the painter of Marrakech".

    When the garden began depends on which of the garden's own publications you read. Its description says he began planting in 1922. Its visitor leaflet says that in 1923 he chose to settle here and bought a large palm grove.

    Its centenary press release dates his purchase of land at the palm grove's edge to 1924. On that count, the garden marked its hundredth anniversary in 2024.

    He conceived it as a sanctuary and a botanical laboratory, planted with specimens from all over the world.
