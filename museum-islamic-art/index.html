  1. 1

    A palace for the arts of Islam

    On Port Said Street in Bab al-Khalq, in front of the Museum of Islamic Art. This is stop 1 on the route.

    Play0:00 / –:––
    Read along

    Look at the building in front of you. Its facade was made in the Mamluk style, borrowing from the buildings of Egypt's medieval sultans. Next door is the National Library of Egypt.

    The idea of a museum for Islamic art first came up under Khedive Ismail, in 1869. It was carried out under his son, Khedive Tawfiq.

    In 1880, an official named Frantz Pasha began to collect objects from the Islamic period. He kept them in the eastern wing of the Mosque of al-Hakim, in the old walled city. The first count was one hundred and eleven pieces.

    A year later, Tawfiq founded a committee to study and protect the Islamic and Coptic monuments of Egypt. Many buildings were falling into ruin. The committee moved important fittings from them into the mosque for safekeeping. In 1892, a small building went up in the mosque's courtyard. Its name was the Arab Museum, and Frantz Pasha ran it.

    The building you see was designed by an Italian architect, Alfonso Manescalo. Khedive Abbas Hilmi the Second opened it on 28 December 1903. For half a century it was known as a museum of Arab art. In 1951 or 1952, it took its present name, the Museum of Islamic Art. The change is linked to the many pieces from the eastern Islamic world that had joined the collection.

    The museum describes itself as the largest museum of Islamic art anywhere.
