    The two colossi

    Read along

    Look up at the two great seated figures in front of you. These are the Colossi of Memnon. Both show one man, the pharaoh Amenhotep the Third. He reigned for thirty-nine years in the fourteenth century BC. Each figure sits upright, with its hands resting on its knees.

    They were never meant to stand alone in a field. They sat at the gate of the First Pylon of the king's great temple on this bank of the Nile. The Egyptians called such a building a house for millions of years. The excavation team calls this one the largest of its kind. Its outer wall enclosed an area about 550 metres by 700 metres. Inside that wall there were smaller temples for other gods. There were processional roads, gardens, pools and workshops. There were storerooms, treasuries and houses for the priests.

    How tall are they? Figures in print vary. The excavators give fourteen to seventeen metres for the royal colossi at the gates. Some accounts round the northern figure up to eighteen.

    The excavators list both of these giants among the quartzite colossi of the temple. Quartzite is a very hard stone, far tougher to carve than the local sandstone.

    Notice how low the ground of the old temple lies. For centuries the river flood spread its mud across the ruins. The excavators found the temple floor buried under Nile mud as much as three metres deep. When they began work, these two figures were the only monuments of the temple standing in their old positions.
