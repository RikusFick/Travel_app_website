  1. 1

    Marcus Simaika and the museum

    In front of the museum entrance, inside the Coptic Cairo compound. This is stop 1 on the route.

    Play0:00 / –:––
    Read along

    This museum holds the largest collection of Coptic artefacts in the world. The Ministry of Tourism and Antiquities says so, and so does the Coptic Orthodox Church.

    The Ministry's visitor rules say audio players are not permitted inside the museum. So each stop of this guide is heard out here. Listen first, then go in and look for yourself.

    The museum began with one man, Marcus Simaika Pasha. He was a leading figure of the Coptic community. He bought and collected Coptic antiquities. He also saved carved pieces from old churches that were being renovated. He used them to build the museum and start its collection.

    The work began in 1908, on land belonging to the Coptic Church. The museum opened in 1910. Until 1931 it belonged to the Coptic Patriarchate. Then it became state property.

    The first building dates from 1910. A second building was opened in 1947.

    Look at the facade. It has been compared to the Fatimid mosque of al-Aqmar, in medieval Cairo. Both are decorated with a carved shell. On the museum, the shell carries a cross.

    The museum gives the size of its collection as about sixteen thousand objects. In 2006, its former director Gawdat Gabra spoke of fourteen thousand. Either way, he said, the aim was never to put everything on display.
