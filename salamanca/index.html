    The Plaza Mayor

    Enter the symmetrical Baroque square designed by Alberto de Churriguera in 1729.

    Around you is the Plaza Mayor of Salamanca, widely regarded as one of the most beautiful squares in Spain. It was built between 1729 and 1755 in the Baroque style, in the warm sandstone that gives Salamanca its nickname, "the golden city".

    King Philip V ordered it, partly as a bullring, and bulls were fought here until the middle of the nineteenth century. It went up in two phases: the first under Alberto de Churriguera, the second under his nephew Manuel de Lara Churriguera. The town hall, with its clock and bell gable, was the work of Andrés García de Quiñones.

    All four sides rise three storeys above a continuous arcade of round arches. On the north side, the town hall stands on five granite arches, and its bell gable is set between four allegorical figures.

    Between the arches runs a series of stone medallions with portraits of the kings of Spain and of famous figures, from conquerors to writers. Some frames were left empty for future names, and new portraits have been added in modern times.

    Every arcade around the square is numbered; locals still arrange to meet "under the clock" or at a given arch.
