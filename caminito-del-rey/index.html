    The Gaitanejo Dam

    Read along

    This small stone building with the big letter S on its front, beside the Gaitanejo dam, is the old Gaitanejo power station, and it explains why the Caminito del Rey exists at all.

    The S stands for the Compañía Sevillana de Electricidad, a later owner. At the start of the twentieth century the Sociedad Hidroeléctrica del Chorro wanted to use the drop of the river Guadalhorce through the Gaitanes gorge to make electricity. A canal was cut from here, partly on the cliffs and partly through tunnels in the rock, down to a power station at El Chorro at the far end of the gorge. Workers needed a way to reach the canal for maintenance, to carry materials and to keep watch over it, so between 1901 and 1905 a narrow path was fixed to the gorge walls. That path is the Caminito.

    The dam in front was planned by the engineer Rafael Benjumea and finished in 1927. It is about 20 metres high, with a crest some 55 metres long, and its power station was built into the dam itself, behind a wall of glass bricks through which the falling water could be seen.

    Across the water, in the soft sandstone of the Sierra Parda, are cave houses dug into the rock. Shepherds and farmers lived in them, and later the dam builders, the canal men and the power station staff. For them the Caminito was the quickest way to the village of El Chorro and its railway station.
