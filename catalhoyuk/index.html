    The experimental house was built to explore Neolithic construction and what living in a Çatalhöyük home might have been like. It combines evidence from several excavated houses rather than copying one particular building.

    Archaeologists worked with builders from Küçükköy and Çumra, drawing on local experience of mudbrick construction. For the brickmaking experiments, they mixed local soils with straw and chaff, moulded the bricks and left them to dry. In one test, most bricks made without plant material cracked as they dried.

    The ancient houses behind this design had no windows. Oak and juniper beams spanned each roof and carried a covering of reeds and clay, while wooden posts, set in pits beside the walls, held the structure up. A typical plan was one large room, often with a smaller side room in which food was stored and prepared.

    The reconstruction included walls given a coat of white plaster and paint, raised platforms, storerooms and an oven below a roof opening. In the excavated houses the large clay oven usually stood on the south wall, immediately under the opening through the roof.

    The roof entrance reflects the ancient houses, reached by ladders from closely packed rooftops. Unlike the excavated Neolithic houses, it was also given an ordinary door for visitors.
