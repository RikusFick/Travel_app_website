  1. 1

    The Main Galleries

    At the entrance to the Main Galleries, on the upper level of the museum (the layout may change; follow the museum's signs). This is stop 1 on the route.

    Play0:00 / –:––
    Read along

    You are standing at the start of a very long story. The museum presents these displays as a walk through Egyptian history, from about 700,000 BC to 394 AD.

    They explore how kings and ordinary people lived from day to day. They also cover what people believed, and how they got ready for burial and the life after death.

    The story runs through four broad periods. The first runs from the prehistoric period to the end of the Old Kingdom and the First Intermediate Period, up to about 2034 BC. The second covers the Middle Kingdom and the Second Intermediate Period, to about 1550 BC. The third is the New Kingdom, to about 1069 BC. The last runs from the Third Intermediate Period through the Late Period to the Graeco-Roman era.

    Three themes run through all of it: Society, Kingship and Beliefs. Society follows the people: hunter-gatherers and farmers, scribes and priests, soldiers and officials. Kingship follows the changing roles of kings and queens over more than three thousand years of royal rule, until Egypt was made a Roman province. Beliefs follows the gods and goddesses, their temples and cults, and the changing customs of burial.

    The Egyptologists John and Colleen Darnell admired this design. They noted that you can view the objects in date order across the themes. Or you can trace a single theme from the earliest times down to Roman rule, then go back and pick up another.

    The museum picks out certain star objects, and this guide stops at a selection of them. Displays change. Objects may be moved, rotated or sent for conservation. If a piece described here is not where you expect it, simply skip that stop.
