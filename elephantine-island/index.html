  1. 1

    The island at the frontier

    At the island's ferry landing, where the boat from the Aswan Corniche ties up. This is stop 1 on the route.

    Play0:00 / –:––
    Read along

    You have crossed the Nile to an island with a very long memory. Elephantine is about a kilometre and a half long and about half a kilometre wide. It lies at the downstream end of the First Cataract, the stretch of rocks and rapids just south of Aswan.

    For the ancient Egyptians, this was the edge of their country. The town on the island faced Aswan across the water. Together they formed Egypt's border town, a base for trade to the south and for military raids. The Ministry of Tourism and Antiquities calls the island the gateway into Egypt for traders coming from Nubia.

    The Egyptian name of the island was Abu, and the Greeks translated it as Elephantine. Both names point to the elephant. Scholars who have mapped the island's sacred sites think the name probably reflects the ivory trade. Ivory was one of many goods that passed through here on its way north.

    People settled here very early. The German Archaeological Institute dates the start of settlement to about 3300 BC. Another study puts the oldest houses at about 3500 BC. So people lived here from somewhere between 3500 and 3300 BC.

    The end came very much later. The German institute says the settlement was given up in the tenth or eleventh century AD. Another study allows that some life went on until the thirteenth or fourteenth century. Either way, the town lasted for more than four thousand years.

    In 1969, the German Archaeological Institute and the Swiss Institute began a joint excavation here. The island's ruins became part of a World Heritage property in 1979.
