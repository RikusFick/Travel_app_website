    This modest building of brick and stone is the Chora, the church of the Holy Saviour of the Chora monastery, known in Turkish as Kariye. Its name means "in the country", the Greek word for countryside. The church stands near the gate later called Edirnekapı.

    Most of what you see grew in layers. The core of the church dates from the eleventh century and was rebuilt in the twelfth. Then, between about 1315 and 1321, the statesman Theodore Metochites added the two entrance halls across this front, the long side chapel on the right, and the mosaics and frescoes that made the Chora famous.

    The building is not large. Its compact plan rises under six domes. In 1453, during the last siege of Constantinople, the city's most revered icon of the Virgin was brought here to protect the nearby walls.

    Around 1500 the church became a mosque. A minaret replaced the bell tower and a mihrab was added, while the pictures were in time plastered over. In 1945 it became a museum, and from the late 1940s American restorers of the Byzantine Institute cleaned the mosaics and frescoes. In 2020 it became a mosque once more.
