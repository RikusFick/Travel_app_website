    Read along

    In front of you rises the largest mosque in the United Arab Emirates. Everything you can see, from the walkway under your feet to the domes high above, was built in a little over a decade. Work began in 1994, and the mosque opened for prayer in 2007.

    The idea came from Sheikh Zayed bin Sultan Al Nahyan, the founding president of the UAE. He wanted a mosque that would bring together the many artistic traditions of the Islamic world in one building, and he followed its design and construction closely. He did not live to see it finished. He died in 2004, three years before it opened, and the mosque carries his name.

    Look at the scale of it. By the mosque centre's own count, the whole site covers more than 555,000 square metres, raised about ten metres above the surrounding land. And the whole building is turned slightly off the street grid. Its main axis points toward the Kaaba in Mecca, the direction every Muslim faces in prayer.

    The style is deliberately mixed. The plan and the arrangement of domes owe a great deal to the seventeenth-century Badshahi Mosque in Lahore, in Pakistan. The arches have a Moorish shape, familiar from North Africa and Spain, and the minarets follow a classic Arab form. The original design was led by the Syrian architect Youssef Abdelke, and craftsmen and materials came from many countries, among them Italy, Germany, Morocco, India, Turkey, China, Greece, the United Kingdom and North Macedonia. More than three thousand workers and 38 contracting companies took part in the building.

    Almost all of what you see is natural material, chosen because it lasts: marble, gold, semi-precious stone, crystal and ceramic. The white you see everywhere is marble from Sivec, near Prilep in North Macedonia. Well over a hundred thousand square metres of it clad the domes, minarets and outer walls. Sources give different totals, from about 115,000 to more than 165,000 square metres, depending on what is counted.
