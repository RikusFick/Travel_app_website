    Two men made this palace, a father and his son, and both served Morocco's sultans. The Ministry's sign calls their family, the Ben Moussa, a leading example of the Makhzen families, households in royal service, and says it served the throne for a century.

    The father, Si Musa, built the first house here in the 1860s, while serving Sultan Muhammad ibn Abd al-Rahman. His son, Ba Ahmed, turned it into a palace.

    When Sultan Hassan I died in 1894, the Ministry's sign says, Ba Ahmed, who had served him, steered the throne to the sultan's son, Abdelaziz.

    He then served the new sultan as grand vizier until his own death in 1900. In those six years he bought the houses around his father's and joined them into this complex. A heritage catalogue says he first made his father's palace bigger on its southern side, then added a string of richly decorated houses, all connected.

    So the palace grew piece by piece, and the result is a maze of around 150 rooms around courtyards and gardens. The whole estate once covered some eight hectares, with stables, a mosque and gardens; the main palace covers almost two. Inside were a bath, treasure rooms and four main courtyards, each different from the others.
