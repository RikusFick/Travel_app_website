    The view of Rocamadour from here shows a village stacked up a cliff face, with chapels above the houses and a castle on top.

    Below runs the canyon of the little river Alzou. People sheltered under these rocks in prehistoric times, and hermits are thought to have lived here from the early Middle Ages. From this hamlet pilgrims followed the path called the Holy Way down into the valley, then up through the village to the sanctuary.

    Documents record a small chapel built in a rock shelter here in 1105, on the boundary between the lands of two Benedictine abbeys, Tulle and Marcilhac. It is often said that the three levels you can see mirror the three orders of medieval society: the knights in the castle at the top, the clergy in the sanctuary in the middle, and the working people down by the river.

    The hamlet's name, L'Hospitalet, comes from the Occitan for a small hospital, a reminder of the shelter it offered pilgrims.
