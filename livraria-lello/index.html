    The Livraria Lello shows the street a narrow Neo-Gothic front of pinnacles and pointed tracery, softened by the flowing lines of Art Nouveau.

    The ground floor is one wide, flattened arch, the kind often called a Tudor arch. The door sits in the middle, with a shop window on either side. Above it run three tall, narrow windows, and beside them stand two painted women. They are Art and Science, painted by Joseph Bielmann, known in Portuguese as José Bielmann, the two guardians of a house of books. Higher still, a lace-like parapet ends in three pinnacles, and the firm's name, Lello and Brother, is painted across the front. In the summer of 2016 conservators finished work on this front and brought back the strong colours it showed in 1906: blues, yellows and a deep wine red.

    The story behind the name starts before this building existed. Ernesto Chardron was a Frenchman, born in 1840, who came to Porto and worked as a clerk in a bookshop. The writer Ramalho Ortigão recorded that Chardron won a large prize in the lottery, eight or ten contos of réis, and used it to set up on his own. In 1869 he opened a bookshop in Porto, and he became a publisher as well. For many years he was the only publisher of Camilo Castelo Branco, one of the great Portuguese novelists of the century, and Eça de Queirós, another great novelist, also appeared under his name. Chardron died young, in 1885, yet the title page of Eça's masterpiece Os Maias, printed in 1888, still carried the name of his house.

    Meanwhile a man named José Pinto de Sousa Lello had opened a bookshop of his own in Porto. In 1894 he bought the old Chardron house, and he ran it with his brother António.

    For their new home the brothers turned to the engineer Francisco Xavier Esteves. The building opened on the thirteenth of January 1906, and the inauguration drew some of the best-known writers and politicians of the day.
