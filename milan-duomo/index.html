    Six centuries in marble

    Begin in Piazza del Duomo facing the towering Candoglia marble façade that took nearly six centuries to complete.

    Read along

    You're looking at the Duomo of Milan, the largest church in Italy, outside the Vatican. From end to end, the outside of the building measures about one hundred and fifty-eight metres.

    Work began in 1386, and it took nearly six hundred years to finish. The last of the great bronze doors was inaugurated in 1965.

    Milan's ruler, Gian Galeazzo Visconti, made two bold choices. Instead of the usual Lombard brick, the cathedral would be built of marble, from the quarry of Candoglia, near Lake Maggiore. And instead of a local style, it would be Gothic, the latest fashion from northern Europe. So the builders brought in engineers, architects and stonecutters from all over the continent.

    To run the project, a body called the Veneranda Fabbrica del Duomo was founded in 1387. It still looks after the cathedral today.

    The façade in front of you took centuries to agree on. It was finally completed between 1807 and 1813, on the orders of Napoleon, who was crowned King of Italy inside this cathedral.

    The marble you see is the same Candoglia marble, from the same quarry, used since the fourteenth century.
