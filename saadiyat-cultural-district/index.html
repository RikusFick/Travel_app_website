  1. 1

    The Abrahamic Family House: the plinth and the raised garden

    On the raised garden of the Abrahamic Family House, where all three houses of worship can be seen. This is stop 1 on the route.

    Play0:00 / –:––
    Read along

    Look around you and count the buildings: a mosque, a church and a synagogue, standing side by side on one shared base. This is the Abrahamic Family House, named for Abraham, the patriarch honoured by Jews, Christians and Muslims alike.

    The idea grew out of a meeting in this city. On 4 February 2019, Pope Francis and the Grand Imam of Al-Azhar, Ahmed el-Tayeb, signed the Document on Human Fraternity in Abu Dhabi, a joint call for peaceful coexistence. Plans for this complex were announced the next day, and the three houses opened together in February 2023.

    The architect was David Adjaye, a Ghanaian-British designer. He started from a simple rule: three cubes equal in volume, each about 30 metres long, 30 metres wide and 30 metres high. From a distance they look like one family. Up close, each has its own façade, its own interior and, most of all, its own direction. Notice that none of them lines up with the others. The mosque turns toward Mecca, the church toward the east and the rising sun, the synagogue toward Jerusalem. The slight twist between them is the faiths' different orientations made visible.

    The walls are wrapped in an off-white concrete chosen to recall the sand and mountains of the Emirates. The cubes rest on a one-storey plinth that belongs to no single religion. Inside it is the Forum, with a central entrance, a library and exhibition space, and each house of worship has its own courtyard with a water feature.

    Where you stand, on the roof of that plinth, is the shared garden. Date palms mark the entrances, and planters hold regional vegetation, with water channels to cool the air. The designers used sunlight as a building material, so each house catches the light at a different time of day. Come back in the late afternoon and the three façades will look quite different from how they look now.
