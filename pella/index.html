    Read along

    Pella was the royal capital of Macedon, and the Ministry of Culture says Alexander the Great was born here.

    It began as a small town on the coast of the Thermaic Gulf.

    Around 400 BC it replaced Aigai as the capital.

    Who moved the court is told two ways.

    The Ephorate of Pella names King Archelaos.

    The Ministry's history leaves it open between Archelaos and the later king Amyntas the Third.

    Poets and artists came from southern Greece to the new court, among them the playwright Euripides and the painter Zeuxis.

    The Ephorate's book says Euripides wrote his Bacchae here.

    How big the city was is also told two ways in the ancient sources.

    The historian Xenophon called Pella the largest city in Macedonia.

    The orator Demosthenes called it small and insignificant, though modern scholars read that as rhetoric.

    Even the name is uncertain.

    It may come from a word for dark, after the colour of the river silt, or from a Macedonian word for stones.

    The whole city covered about four square kilometres.
