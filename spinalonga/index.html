    Read along

    Spinalonga is a bare, dry rock of about eight and a half hectares, lying across the mouth of the Elounda harbour.

    In antiquity a fort stood here to guard Olous, the old city of the bay.

    Hellenic Heritage gives the islet an ancient name, Kalydon.

    The name Spinalonga is explained in two ways.

    Hellenic Heritage reads it as the Latin spina lunga, meaning a long thorn.

    The Ephorate of Antiquities of Lasithi tells another story.

    Venetian scribes wrote down the Greek words eis tin Elounda, towards Elounda, in forms such as stinalonde.

    From those sounds the Venetians shaped the name Spinalonga, for both the harbour and the rock at its mouth.

    The Ephorate records a sea battle in this gulf in 1204, won by Venice against Genoa.

    The gulf had salt pans too, and Hellenic Heritage says their salt went to Europe.

    By the Ephorate's account, they gained importance once Venice lost Cyprus and its salt works, in 1571.

    The pirate Hayreddin Barbarossa had plundered the eastern coasts of the island in the late 1530s.

    So the Venetian Senate resolved to fortify the islet against enemy fleets and pirates.

    Venice came to rank this harbour second only to Souda among the natural harbours of Crete.

    Once the fort was built, the Ephorate notes, pirate raids fell sharply, and the empty shores of the gulf were farmed and settled.
