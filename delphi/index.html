    The Roman Agora

    On the paved square just inside the site entrance, below the first stretch of the Sacred Way. This is stop 1 on the route.

    Free sample: stop 1

    Play0:00 / –:––
    Read along

    This paved square at the entrance is known as the Roman Agora, the first ruin on the way into the sanctuary. Above you rises the sanctuary of Apollo, at the foot of Mount Parnassus, in the angle formed by the twin cliffs of the Phaedriades. It had the most famous oracle of ancient Greece, and for many centuries it was the religious centre and a symbol of unity for the Greek world.

    The story begins in myth. At first the place was sacred to Mother Earth and guarded by a terrible serpent, the Python, which Apollo later killed. The cult of Apollo was established in the eighth century BC, and the sanctuary and its oracle grew from then on.

    After antiquity a village called Kastri grew up over the ruins. In 1891 the Greek government gave the French School at Athens permission to dig here. The houses of Kastri were taken over and demolished, and the "Great Excavation" began in 1892. It uncovered spectacular remains, including about three thousand inscriptions.
