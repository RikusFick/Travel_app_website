    The Sanctuary of Bom Jesus do Monte, Good Jesus of the Mount, begins at this gateway, and from here a Baroque stairway climbs the hill towards the church, a rise of about 116 metres.

    The hill is Mount Espinho, facing west over Braga. A chapel of the Holy Cross is recorded on it as early as 1373, and in 1494 the Archbishop of Braga, Jorge da Costa, had another chapel built here. In 1629 a group of devout men founded the Confraternity of Bom Jesus do Monte, which put up a chapel with an image of Christ crucified and the first small chapels of the Passion. The same confraternity has looked after the sanctuary ever since, for almost four hundred years.

    The sanctuary you see today dates from the 1720s, when another Archbishop of Braga, Rodrigo de Moura Teles, set out to rebuild it. His coat of arms is over this gate, and an inscription at the entrance reads: Holy Jerusalem, restored and rebuilt in the year 1723.

    His idea belonged to a European tradition, the sacred mountain, or Sacro Monte, encouraged by the Catholic Church after the Council of Trent in the sixteenth century, in answer to the Protestant Reformation. A whole hill was shaped into a recreation of Jerusalem and the road to Calvary, so that pilgrims could climb through the story of Christ's Passion towards a church at the top.

    The building material is the local granite. Walls, steps, fountains and statues are all granite, and the walls between them are plastered and whitewashed, so that the white and the grey stand out against the green of the woods around. The whole enclosure, woods and park included, covers some twenty-six hectares, and it is open to anyone who wants to climb.
