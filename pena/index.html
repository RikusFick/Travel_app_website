    Monumental Gate

    Read along

    The Monumental Gate of the Pena Palace is a triumphal arch in stone, the first threshold of a palace built to look like a castle out of a romance.

    On this hilltop, in the Middle Ages, a small chapel was raised to Our Lady of Pena after reports that the Virgin had appeared here. In 1503 King Manuel the First founded a monastery on the site for the monks of the Order of Saint Jerome, the same order he installed at Jerónimos in Lisbon. It was a small and quiet house, built for no more than eighteen monks.

    In the 1700s lightning struck the monastery, and then the earthquake of 1755 left it badly damaged. In 1834 Portugal dissolved its religious orders, and the monks left. Four years later, in 1838, the ruin was bought by King Ferdinand the Second, a German prince of the house of Saxe-Coburg and Gotha, husband of Queen Maria the Second, and known in Portugal as the King-Artist. He bought the monastery with the lands around it.

    This gate quotes the Portuguese buildings of the 1500s, the century of the monastery. The sentry boxes on either side copy the domed turrets of the Belém Tower. The diamond-shaped reliefs on the façade imitate those of the Casa dos Bicos, a Lisbon house of the same century. Just beyond the arch, a drawbridge leads into a tunnel that climbs to the upper courtyards: a defence that was meant not to defend anything, but to evoke the castles of the Middle Ages.
