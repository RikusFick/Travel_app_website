    The Couesnon Dam

    This part of the route looks at The Couesnon Dam.

    Read along

    The Couesnon Dam, this long walkway across the river, was built between 2006 and 2009 to help keep Mont-Saint-Michel an island.

    Over the nineteenth and twentieth centuries, sand and silt built up around the Mount, helped by the causeway road of 1879, which blocked the currents. The dam uses the force of the tides and of the river: it holds back water as the tide comes in, then releases it at low tide, flushing sediment away from the rock. Its walkway was also designed as a viewpoint over the bay.

    In 2014 the old causeway was replaced by the light bridge that now leads to the Mount, set on slender piles so that the sea can flow freely around it.

    The dam replaced an older one of the 1960s, which had six gates opened by hand. It was built to protect the town of Pontorson upstream from floods, but it held the water back without flushing it, and it made the silting worse, above and below its gates.

    The new dam works with eight curved steel gates, each moved by two hydraulic rams, and the gates can work in both directions. About an hour and a half before high tide the gates close, so that the first, muddiest surge of the sea stops against them and drops its sediment. Shortly before high tide they open and the cleaner water pours over the top to fill the river behind. When the tide turns they close again, holding back a reservoir that can run, depending on the season and the tide, from tens of thousands to more than a million cubic metres. About six hours after high tide, at low water, the gates open slowly from below. The released water runs out for anywhere from half an hour to three hours, without a wave, and carries the sand and silt out towards the open sea.

    The last of the eight gates was set in place in 2008, and the dam has been in service since 2009. A lock with two fish passes lets salmon and other fish move between the river and the bay. The walkway on top, by the architect Luc Weizmann, is decked in wood and is said to have been designed to recall the deck of a ship.
