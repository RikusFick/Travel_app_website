    Bethany beyond the Jordan

    Where the shuttle sets you down, at the start of the walking trail, beside the ancient pool. This is stop 1 on the route.

    Play0:00 / –:––
    Read along

    Listen for a moment. Reeds, birds, the wind in the trees. You are in the Zor, the strip of green wilderness along the River Jordan, about nine kilometres north of the Dead Sea, in one of the lowest places on earth.

    The Gospel of John names this place. "These things took place in Bethany beyond the Jordan, where John was baptising" (John 1:28). Here John the Baptist preached repentance, and here Jesus of Nazareth came to be baptised by him. Most Christian churches honour this east bank of the river as that place, and in 2015 UNESCO inscribed it as a World Heritage Site under the name Baptism Site "Bethany Beyond the Jordan", Al-Maghtas. In Arabic, al-Maghtas means the place of immersion.

    For a long time this wilderness was closed to pilgrims. After the war of 1967 the river became a front line, and both banks were sealed off and heavily mined. Then, in 1994, Jordan and Israel signed a peace treaty. Soon after, Prince Ghazi of Jordan visited the area with the Franciscan archaeologist Father Michele Piccirillo, who was convinced that the baptism site lay here. They found remains of Roman and Byzantine buildings, and that was enough. The land was cleared of mines and the excavations began.

    What the archaeologists found matches what pilgrims had written down fifteen centuries ago: churches, chapels, a monastery, hermit caves and pools for baptism. In 2002 the site opened for daily visits. In 2000 Pope John Paul II visited the site, followed by Pope Benedict the Sixteenth in 2009 and Pope Francis in 2014.
