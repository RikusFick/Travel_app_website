  1. 1

    A small museum with a clear idea

    In the garden in front of the Luxor Museum, on the Nile corniche in Luxor. This is stop 1 on the route.

    Play0:00 / –:––
    Read along

    You are in Luxor, which grew on the site of ancient Thebes. Thebes was the capital of Egypt for much of the New Kingdom, roughly 1550 to 1070 BC. This museum stands on the corniche, looking across the Nile. It was built around a simple idea: show less, and show it well.

    The museum was planned in 1962, when the architect Mahmoud El Hakim was asked to design it. It opened in 1975. Compared with the great museums of Cairo, the range of objects here is deliberately limited. The aim was fine pieces, shown without clutter, with clear labels.

    Counts of the collection differ. Counts of the selected pieces range from about twelve hundred to around twenty-two hundred. Either way, it is a size you can take in calmly.

    Look at the garden. It has held colossal statues of kings, among them Ramesses the Second, Amenhotep the Third and Merenptah. Statues here are sometimes moved, so the line-up can change from one year to the next.

    Most of the museum's objects were found in the area of ancient Thebes, on both banks of the river, close to where you stand.
