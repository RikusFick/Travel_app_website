    Read along

    The Porta Férrea, the Iron Gate, is the ceremonial entrance to the University of Coimbra, given its monumental form in 1634.

    Its sculptures represent the four old faculties, Medicine, Law, Theology and Canon Law, along with Wisdom, the emblem of the university, and two kings: Dinis, who founded the university, and João the Third, who settled it for good in Coimbra.

    King Dinis signed the founding charter on 1 March 1290, and Pope Nicholas the Fourth confirmed it that year. But the university began in Lisbon, not here. For more than two centuries it moved back and forth between Lisbon and Coimbra, until João the Third brought it to Coimbra for good in 1537. Within a few years all its faculties were housed in the royal palace.

    That palace stands on the site of an old Islamic fortress, the citadel of Coimbra. After the Christian conquest, Portugal's first king, Afonso Henriques, made it his royal palace, and several kings of the first dynasty were born here. In 1597 the university bought the building from the crown, and since then it has been called the Paço das Escolas, the Palace of the Schools.
