  1. 1

    The First Pylon and the Battle of Kadesh

    At the ruined gateway at the front of the temple, looking in towards the first court. This is stop 1 on the route.

    Play0:00 / –:––
    Read along

    You stand before the monumental entrance of a king's memorial temple. Egyptians called it the House of Millions of Years, naming it for Ramesses the Second under his throne name, Usermaatra-setepenra.

    The name Ramesseum came much later. French decipherer Jean-François Champollion coined it after visiting the ruins in 1829 and reading the king's cartouches across the stone.

    In front of you stood a pylon, the massive tapering gateway of an Egyptian temple. The French excavation team gives it as about sixty-nine metres long and around twenty-two metres high. The French team believes it probably carried two flagmasts on each of its two towers. In ancient times, it says, a canal probably brought boats from the Nile to a quay in front of this gate. Everything that lay in front of it has disappeared under farmland.

    Look at what survives. The face turned towards the courtyard has weathered better, carrying the defining military tale of this reign: the Battle of Kadesh.

    In his fifth regnal year, around 1274 BC, Ramesses marched north into Syria to confront King Muwatalli of the Hittites along the Orontes.

    Reliefs show the pharaoh driving his chariot in person while the fortress surrenders, celebrating how he broke through enemy ranks with his guard.

    That account is the king's own, and it is boastful. The battle was not decisive. The town was not captured, and the fighting went on for several more years. The same pylon shows towns recaptured in the eighth year. One scene records the pillage of a town called Shalem in his eighth year. Many think it was Jerusalem, but that is not certain.

    This gate was also meant to be a barrier. The French team describes the pylon as the last barrier between the ordered world inside the temple and the chaos outside. The Egyptians saw their monuments as charged with magical power.
