    Before the tombs, look up at this gate, Bab Agnaou. Sources disagree on its date.

    An architectural archive says the founding Almohad caliph, Abd al-Mumin, built it in 1147. In its account, the gate rose to importance only when the Almohad kasbah was built nearby in 1185.

    A Spanish atlas of Almohad architecture credits it instead to Yaqub al-Mansur, the third Almohad caliph. In 1185, it says, he began a new kasbah at the southern edge of the medina, and moved the seat of power there from the old palace beside the Koutoubia Mosque.

    Through this gate, the people of Marrakesh reached the royal kasbah and its mosque, the quarter where the Saadian burial ground stands. The archive says this is the city's one surviving gate from Almohad times.

    The atlas adds that the gate did not open straight into the city. It faced a walled square, about a hundred metres by thirty-five, that may have served for ceremonies.

    The opening is a wide horseshoe arch, set in stone carved in rings of geometric patterns that circle the opening. Above it, the corner panels carry finer carving of plants and leaves, and a carved shell sits at the heart of each. A broad frame of Quranic inscriptions in stone runs around it all, and above it sits a thick carved stone cornice with geometric patterns.

    Look at the colours, too: red and grey-blue stones are mixed together, with more of the grey-blue at the bottom and the top.
